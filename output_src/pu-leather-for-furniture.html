PU Leather for Sofas and Furniture Upholstery

For residential sofas and commercial upholstery, specify PU leather between 1.2–1.8 mm thick with a weight of 800–1,200 g/m² and Martindale abrasion resistance of at least 40,000 cycles (ISO 5470-2). Any grade below that will delaminate or wear through within two years of daily use. At Demo Leather Co., Ltd., we ship over 2,500 linear meters per month of furniture-grade PU to upholstery manufacturers in Europe, Southeast Asia, and North America — and we track every batch through our own QC lab.

This guide gives you the exact specifications to request from your supplier, the trade-offs between budget and durability, and the test reports you should ask to see before placing an order.

What thickness of PU leather is best for sofas?

Heavy upholstery applications need 1.2–1.8 mm thickness combined with a backing fabric weight of 800–1,200 g/m². Thinner PU (0.8–1.2 mm) is used for bags and footwear and will not survive the shear load of a seated person sliding against a cushion.

Application Recommended Thickness Weight Range Martindale (ISO 5470-2)
Residential sofas (light use) 1.2–1.4 mm 800–1,000 g/m² 15k–25k cycles
Residential sofas (daily use) 1.4–1.6 mm 900–1,100 g/m² 25k–40k cycles
Commercial / hospitality seating 1.5–1.8 mm 1,000–1,200 g/m² 40k–80k cycles
Contract / high-traffic waiting areas 1.6–1.8 mm 1,100–1,200 g/m² ≥80k cycles

Our recommendation for general furniture importers: start at 1.4 mm / 1,000 g/m² with a knit-polyester backing. This handles daily household use for 5–7 years. If your buyer is a hotel chain or a restaurant group, move to 1.6 mm / 1,100 g/m² with a woven backing and specify ≥50k Martindale.

What is the 4-grade durability ladder for PU upholstery?

We classify every furniture-grade PU we produce into four grades based on real field returns and lab data. We call this the Demo Durability Ladder — ask your supplier for their equivalent grading.

  1. Economy (Grade C). 1.2 mm, polyester-based PU, dry-process only. Martindale ~15k. Hydrolysis resistance: 2–3 years (ISO 1419 Method C). Suitable for promotional furniture, short-term rentals, or seasonal collections. Expect peeling at seam creases by year 3.
  2. Standard (Grade B). 1.4 mm, polyester-based PU with anti-hydrolysis additives. Martindale 25k–40k. Hydrolysis resistance: 4–5 years. This is the most common grade we ship to mid-range sofa manufacturers in Southeast Asia.
  3. Premium (Grade A). 1.5–1.6 mm, polycarbonate/polyether PU (higher hydrolysis resistance than polyester). Martindale 50k–80k. Hydrolysis resistance: 7–10 years. Backing is woven or high-density knit. Suitable for EU-brand residential lines and boutique hotels.
  4. Commercial (Grade AA). 1.6–1.8 mm, polyether PU with self-healing topcoat. Martindale ≥80k. Passes 50k+ cycles at −30 to +80 °C (ISO 5402 flex test). Hydrolysis resistance: 10+ years. Used by airline lounges, cruise ships, and government contract furniture.

Honest note: No PU leather "lasts forever." Even Grade AA will eventually hydrolyze because the PU micropore coating (the 0.05–0.15 mm skin) is a polymer that breaks down in humid heat. What you pay for is how many years before that happens.

How do I choose between PU, PVC, microfiber, or genuine leather for furniture?

There is no single "best" material — the right choice depends on your climate, budget, and durability target. Here is our honest comparison based on what we see in actual orders:

Material Cost rank (1 = cheapest per m²) Breathability Hydrolysis risk Typical lifespan (sofa use) Best for
PVC leather 1 Low (non-porous) Low (plasticizer migration instead) 3–5 years Budget commercial seating, bar stools
PU leather (standard) 2 Medium (microporous coating) Moderate (polyester-based) 4–5 years Mid-range residential sofas
PU leather (premium) 3 Good (polyether/polycarbonate PU) Low (engineered for humid markets) 7–10 years EU-brand residential, hotels
Microfiber (超纤) 4 Excellent (sea-island ≤0.3 denier) Very low 10+ years (~200k flex cycles) High-end automotive, luxury contract
Genuine leather (full-grain) 5 Excellent None (protein-based, different decay mechanism) 15+ years with care High-end residential, luxury hospitality

Our factory's observation: For a B2B furniture buyer importing to a tropical or subtropical market (e.g., Vietnam, Philippines, Brazil, Middle East), premium PU or microfiber is safer than standard PU. Polyester-based PU in 80%+ humidity at 30 °C can fail in under 3 years. We have seen the returns firsthand.

What backing fabric should furniture-grade PU have?

Three backing types are standard: knit, woven, and non-woven. For furniture, woven backing gives the best dimensional stability and seam strength.

  • Knit polyester (circular knit). Stretches ~20–30%. Good for curved sofa arms and tight-radius corners. Most common in residential 1.2–1.4 mm PU. Lower tear resistance (ASTM D2261: ~40–50 N).
  • Woven polyester. Stretches ~5–10%. Preferred for commercial upholstery. Holds staples better, resists seam slippage. Tear resistance ≥60 N. Slightly higher cost (+$0.30–0.50/m²).
  • Non-woven (polyester or recycled PET). Lowest cost. Typically used in economy-grade 1.2 mm PU. Acceptable for vertical surfaces (sofa back), not recommended for seating panels.
  • Microfiber (超纤) — sea-island structure. Only used in premium and automotive grades. Softest hand, highest tear strength (~80 N+).

We can supply any of these. For a first-time furniture buyer, we recommend starting with 1.4 mm PU on woven polyester backing, litchi grain, topcoated with a silicone hand-feel finish. This is our most reordered specification across 14 countries.

What certifications should furniture-grade PU have?

Every shipment we make includes the following certifications, and your supplier should provide them without extra charges:

  • REACH (EU) — DMF content <0.1 ppm, SVHC below threshold. Mandatory for any European furniture import.
  • RoHS — Heavy metals and phthalate compliance.
  • California Prop 65 — Required for US-market furniture.
  • OEKO-TEX Standard 100 — Class II (articles in direct contact with skin). Our standard grade carries this.
  • ISO 1419 Method C (hydrolysis). Request the 4-week accelerated test report in writing. A grade that claims "anti-hydrolysis" but cannot show you the ISO 1419 report is a red flag.

Our standard lead time for a hydrolysis-tested batch is 20–25 days (vs. 15 days for stock non-tested rolls). Full test reports from SGS or Intertek are available on request with each PO.

What are the MOQ, width, and lead time for furniture-grade PU?

Standard MOQ is 1,000 linear meters per color per thickness for PU; 300 m for microfiber grades. These are realistic numbers from a production-run perspective — any supplier quoting 100 m MOQ for custom-grain PU is likely splitting a stock roll, not making fresh stock for you.

  • Width: 54–58 inches (1.37–1.47 m) standard.
  • Roll size: 30–50 m per roll, depending on thickness.
  • Lead time from stock: 15 days for standard colors (black, dark brown, cream, gray).
  • Lead time custom color / custom grain: 20–25 days (hydrolysis-tested grade: +5–10 days).

Common buyer question: Why does PU leather peel on sofas?

Peeling on a sofa is almost always hydrolysis of the PU coating — the polymer breaks down in high humidity and heat, loses adhesion to the backing, and flakes off. We see this most often on budget-grade polyester-based PU (Grade C) shipped to tropical markets without warning the buyer.

The fix is not to "repair" the peeling. The fix is to specify polycarbonate or polyether PU (Grade A or AA) and ask for the ISO 1419 Method C report before you buy. A 4-week accelerated test equivalent to 5 years in 30 °C / 80% RH will tell you if the material is suitable for your climate.

How do I care for PU leather furniture?

Clean with mild soap and water only. No solvents, no alcohol-based cleaners, no abrasive pads. Solvents attack the PU micropore coating and accelerate hydrolysis.

  • Wipe spills immediately with a soft damp cloth.
  • For routine cleaning: a few drops of mild dish soap in water, dampen a microfiber cloth, wipe the surface, then wipe again with a clean damp cloth.
  • Keep furniture out of direct sunlight. UV accelerates PU degradation regardless of grade. Our automotive grades (SAE J2527, ΔE<1.5 at 1,000 h) are UV-stabilized, but standard furniture grades are not.
  • Do not use leather conditioner or saddle soap — those are formulated for protein-based genuine leather and can damage PU coating.
