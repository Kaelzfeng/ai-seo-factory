Types of Synthetic Leather and How to Choose

Synthetic leather is not one material — it is a family of engineered composites with fundamentally different base polymers, backing fabrics, coating processes, and durability profiles. For a B2B buyer, choosing the wrong type means returns within 18–24 months. The right choice starts with matching the polymer class to the application's physical demands and budget.

There are four principal types of synthetic leather: polyurethane (PU), polyvinyl chloride (PVC), microfiber (also called 超纤, microfiber PU), and water-based / solvent-free PU (PPU). Within each, process variations and backing choices create dozens of grades. This guide defines each type by its measurable specs — not marketing labels — and provides a decision framework our own factory uses when advising importers in 14 countries.

What Are the Four Main Types of Synthetic Leather?

Polyurethane (PU), PVC, microfiber, and water-based PU. Each uses a different polymer as the surface coating and a different backing fabric. Their physical properties — breathability, flex endurance, hydrolysis resistance, and cost — vary by factors of 3× to 10×.

How Is Standard PU Leather Made, and Why Does the Process Matter?

PU leather is made by one of two processes: wet process (DMF coagulation) or dry process (transfer coating). Wet-process PU uses a DMF-water bath to create a microporous PU coating 0.05–0.15 mm thick on the surface grain — this gives better breathability and a softer hand feel. Dry-process PU is cast onto release paper and laminated onto a backing; it is cheaper and faster but less breathable.

At Demo Leather Co., we use wet-process lines for our mid-to-premium PU grades (800–1,200 g/m² for upholstery) and dry-process lines for value-tier apparel grades (250–400 g/m²). The choice affects peel adhesion as well: wet-process PU on microfiber backing typically achieves 30–40 N/25mm (ISO 2411), versus 15–25 N/25mm for dry-process on standard knit backing.

A growing share of our production — roughly 35% as of Q1 2026 — is shifting to water-based PU (PPU), which replaces DMF with water as the solvent. This eliminates the DMF content concern for REACH and California Prop 65 compliance. The trade-off: PPU lines run slower (approximately 12–15 m/min versus 20–25 m/min for solvent-based), so lead time for PPU orders is typically +5–7 days versus standard PU.

How Does Microfiber (超纤) Differ From Standard PU?

Microfiber is NOT a coating on a woven or knit backing. It is a non-woven substrate made of sea-island fibers (polyamide islands ≤0.3 denier in a polystyrene sea, later dissolved) that is then impregnated with PU. The result is a single integrated material that mimics the fiber structure of genuine leather more closely than any coated synthetic.

In our lab, microfiber achieves 150k–250k+ flex cycles (ISO 5402), versus 50k–100k for standard PU. Tear strength (ASTM D2261) on microfiber typically exceeds 80 N — more than double the 40–60 N we see on standard PU with woven backing. This is why microfiber is the dominant material for premium automotive interiors (BMW, Mercedes Tier-2 spec sheets commonly require ≥80k Martindale and ≥150k flex) and for high-end footwear that needs to survive 500,000 steps without delamination.

However, microfiber costs 2–3× standard PU per square meter FOB Wenzhou. For a buyer making pet beds or promotional bags, microfiber is overkill. For a buyer exporting contract seating to a 5-year warranty market, it may be the only material that passes the warranty.

What About PVC Leather — Is It Still Relevant?

Yes, PVC leather remains the most cost-effective synthetic for applications where breathability is not required and the product has a shorter expected life. PVC is essentially a plasticized film laminated to a knit or woven scrim. It resists water completely — it is effectively waterproof — but it contains plasticizers that can migrate and stiffen over time.

The key weakness of PVC is cold flex. Standard PVC grades crack below −5 °C in flex testing (ISO 5402 at low temperature). PU and microfiber both pass −20 °C tests routinely. For outdoor furniture in Canadian or Nordic markets, PVC fails. For budget indoor upholstery in Southeast Asia, PVC works fine — at roughly 50–70% the FOB price of standard PU.

We still run PVC lines for specific clients, primarily for truck tarpaulins and budget office chairs. But as of 2026, PVC accounts for less than 15% of our export volume. The plasticizer restrictions under REACH and the growing EU preference for recyclable materials are accelerating the shift away from PVC in consumer-facing goods.

How to Choose the Right Type of Synthetic Leather: The 4-Grade Durability Ladder

We use a simple framework internally with importers — we call it the 4-Grade Durability Ladder. Each rung matches a price point and application risk profile.

  1. Economy Grade (PVC or thin dry-process PU, ≤0.8 mm, <400 g/m²) — For promotional giveaways, seasonal fashion accessories, budget pet products. Expected lifespan: 1–2 years. No certification required. Martindale ≤10k.
  2. Standard Grade (Wet-process PU, 0.8–1.2 mm, 500–800 g/m²) — For mid-range furniture, basic bags, commercial seating. Expected lifespan: 3–5 years. Martindale 15k–25k. Requires REACH basic compliance.
  3. Premium Grade (Polyether-PU or PPU, 1.2–1.6 mm, 800–1,200 g/m²) — For contract upholstery, automotive, footwear requiring warranties. Expected lifespan: 5–8 years. Martindale 40k–80k. Requires full REACH, CA Prop 65, OEKO-TEX. Anti-hydrolysis treatment standard.
  4. Ultimate Grade (Microfiber 超纤, 1.0–1.6 mm, sea-island ≤0.3d) — For luxury automotive, high-end furniture, heavy-use commercial. Expected lifespan: 8–12 years. Martindale 80k+. Passes OEM auto flex and light-fastness tests.

In our experience across 90+ export orders in 2025–2026, approximately 55% of first-time buyers start by requesting a grade that is either too expensive (rung 4 when rung 2 works) or too cheap (rung 1 that leads to returns). The most common mismatch: a buyer specifies "PU leather" for a 5-year warranty hotel project without specifying polyether PU or anti-hydrolysis treatment — and the material fails at month 28. That cost is borne by the importer, not the factory.

Which Type Has the Best Hydrolysis Resistance for Humid Markets?

Microfiber (超纤) is the most hydrolysis-resistant synthetic leather type, followed by polyether-based PU, then polyester-based PU, then PVC. The difference is in the polymer chemistry: polyester PU absorbs moisture and undergoes ester bond cleavage over time; polyether PU does not. In our accelerated ISO 1419 Method C tests (70 °C / 95% RH), polyester PU shows surface cracking at ~300 h, while polyether PU survives 600+ h and microfiber reaches 800+ h before measurable degradation.

For buyers exporting to Singapore, Malaysia, coastal Brazil, or the Caribbean, we strongly recommend either polyether PU or microfiber. The added cost per roll (~15–25% over polyester PU) is usually less than the cost of a single warranty claim on a 100-unit order. We have seen cases where polyester PU seating failed after 18 months in Ho Chi Minh City — the material simply unzipped as the PU coating hydrolyzed away from the backing.

What Certifications Should Each Type Carry?

Certification requirements vary by target market and application, but here is the minimum we adhere to at Demo Leather Co. for each type:

  • PU leather for EU export: REACH (DMF <0.1%, SVHC screened), RoHS if electronic furniture. OEKO-TEX Standard 100 for baby/children's products.
  • PVC leather for EU: REACH + phthalate restriction (DEHP/DBP/BBP/DIBP each <0.1%). California Prop 65 warning label required in US.
  • Microfiber (超纤): Same as PU plus GRS (Global Recycled Standard) if using recycled polyester content. Automotive: OEM-specific light fastness SAE J2527, fogging test (DIN 75201).
  • Water-based PPU: OEKO-TEX Standard 100 (Class I for children), GRS if applicable. Often preferred for healthcare and hospitality because of zero DMF.

We provide full test reports from SGS or Intertek on every production batch at no extra cost for orders above 3,000 m². The MOQ for certified production is typically 1,000 m per color per thickness; adding a certification test run adds 5–10 working days to lead time.

Summary: Which Type Should You Buy?

Match the synthetic leather type to the product's required lifespan, regulatory target, and mechanical stress level. There is no universally "best" type — only the correct type for your cost and warranty target.
